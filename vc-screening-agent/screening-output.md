# MeridianOps — First-Pass VC Screen

## Executive summary

**WATCH.** MeridianOps fits the enterprise-AI / applied-AI thesis and shows potentially strong growth and retention, but the current materials leave several underwriting-critical questions unanswered. The strongest signal is reported $18M ARR growing 85% YoY with 121% NRR. The biggest concern is whether its insurance workflow library and data create a durable moat versus vertical SaaS incumbents and increasingly capable general-purpose AI automation tools.

## Competitive / moat assessment

The key investment question is whether MeridianOps is becoming the system of record for insurance operations or simply an AI-powered automation layer. A workflow library is useful but may not be durable if competitors can reproduce workflows with similar foundation models. Stronger evidence would include proprietary process data, unusually high implementation success, customer-specific workflow depth, high switching costs, superior accuracy on insurance tasks, or expansion into increasingly mission-critical workflows.

## Key risks

1. AI inference costs could pressure gross margins as automation volume scales.
2. General-purpose agent platforms may compress differentiation.
3. Customer concentration and retention quality are unknown.
4. Implementation burden could constrain growth and margins.
5. The $30M ARR target may depend on pipeline assumptions not provided.

## Five diligence questions

1. What do logo retention and NRR look like by cohort, customer size, and vintage?
2. What percentage of ARR comes from the top 10 customers, and how concentrated is expansion?
3. What are current gross margins and AI/inference costs per workflow, and how do they trend with scale?
4. Why do customers choose MeridianOps over incumbent insurance software plus general AI automation tools, and what measurable switching cost develops after deployment?
5. Can management provide customer references demonstrating that the workflow/data layer produces materially better outcomes than alternatives?

## Recommendation

**WATCH — advance only after targeted diligence.** The reported growth profile is strong enough to warrant attention, and the company is squarely within the thesis. However, the moat, economics, customer quality, and geography are not yet sufficiently evidenced. A strong customer-reference set plus cohort economics and proof that workflow/data advantages improve retention or performance would move this toward **ADVANCE**.
